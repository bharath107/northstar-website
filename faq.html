Getting started

Do I need to know German before I start?

No. Many doctors start from zero. We plan your route to B2, the general German level most states require, and on to medical German.

Can I start during my internship?

Yes, and it is a good idea. Language takes the longest, so starting early saves time later.

How long does the whole pathway take?

Usually between 8 months and 2 years. It depends on your starting German, how many hours you can study, your documents, exam dates and the state you apply to.

Is the consultation really free?

Yes. It is a one to one call where we look at your profile and suggest a plan. There is no obligation to join.

Language and exams

What German level do I need?

B2 in general German, and medical German at B2 to C1 level, which is tested in the Fachsprachprüfung (FSP). See our glossary for what the exam involves.

What is the FSP?

The Fachsprachprüfung is a medical German exam of about 60 to 80 minutes in three parts: taking a patient’s history (Anamnese), writing a medical report (Arztbrief) and discussing the case with another doctor. The fee is usually €400 to €600 depending on the state. FSP preparation is available with Northstar for an extra fee.

What is the Kenntnisprüfung?

An oral and practical exam that checks your medical knowledge against the German standard. Most doctors with a degree from outside the EU, including India, are asked to take it.

Documents, visa and licence

Which documents will I need?

Usually your degree certificate, mark sheets or transcript, internship certificate, proof of registration in India, passport, CV, police clearance and a medical certificate, plus certified translations. The exact list depends on the state.

Which German state should I choose?

It depends on your documents, your timeline and where you want to live, because each state handles applications differently. State selection is part of the package.

What is the difference between Approbation and Berufserlaubnis?

The Approbation is your full, permanent licence, valid everywhere in Germany. A Berufserlaubnis is a temporary permit to work under supervision, usually for up to two years. Time worked on a Berufserlaubnis generally does not count towards your specialist training.

Working in Germany

How much will I earn?

In a municipal hospital a first year resident earns about €5,700 a month gross, rising with experience and specialisation. See the salary table on our home page.

Do you guarantee a job?

No one honestly can. We prepare you fully and support your applications; the result depends on your licence, language level, the state and hospital openings.

Is specialist training paid?

Yes. Residency (Weiterbildung) in Germany is a salaried job, not a paid course.
