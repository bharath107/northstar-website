Glossary

Words you will hear often

The German system has its own vocabulary. These five terms come up again and again on your way to working as a doctor in Germany.

Fachsprachprüfung FSP

The Fachsprachprüfung (FSP) is a medical German language exam that tests your ability to communicate with patients and colleagues, take patients’ medical histories and write medical reports. It is an important step towards obtaining your medical licence in Germany.

Read moreShow less

The Fachsprachprüfung is a B2 to C1 level medical language examination for international doctors who want to work in Germany. It usually takes around 60 to 80 minutes and consists of three parts:

  • Anamnese: taking a patient’s medical history (approx. 20 minutes)
  • Arztbrief: writing a medical report or discharge summary (approx. 20 to 30 minutes)
  • Arzt-Arzt-Gespräch: presenting and discussing the case with another doctor (approx. 20 minutes)

The examination fee is usually between €400 and €600, depending on the federal state. If you fail, you can generally repeat the exam. The main aim is to ensure that international doctors can communicate safely and confidently in a German clinical environment.

Berufserlaubnis

The Berufserlaubnis is a temporary medical work permit that allows international doctors to work in German hospitals under supervision while completing the remaining requirements for their Approbation.

Read moreShow less

The Berufserlaubnis allows international doctors to start working in Germany as doctors under supervision, even before receiving their full medical licence (Approbation). It is generally issued for a maximum period of two years, although the exact conditions may vary depending on the federal state.

During this time, you can gain valuable clinical experience, improve your medical German and become familiar with the German healthcare system. You can work as an Assistenzarzt within the limits of your permit and the conditions set by the responsible authority.

Important: the time worked under a Berufserlaubnis generally does not count towards your specialist medical training (Facharztweiterbildung).

The Berufserlaubnis is therefore an important step towards building your medical career in Germany.

Kenntnisprüfung KP

The Kenntnisprüfung (KP) is a medical examination that assesses whether your medical knowledge and clinical skills meet German standards. It is an important final step towards obtaining your Approbation as an international doctor.

Read moreShow less

The Kenntnisprüfung is an oral and practical medical examination conducted entirely in German before a state examination commission. The exam itself generally lasts around 60 to 90 minutes, but it can sometimes take an entire day.

It focuses mainly on Internal Medicine and Surgery, along with emergency medicine, pharmacology and radiology. The examination includes taking a patient’s medical history (Anamnese), performing a physical examination and preparing a medical report (Arztbrief). You will also need to discuss your diagnosis, differential diagnoses and proposed treatment with the examiners.

Unlike India, Germany does not have a central postgraduate entrance examination for residency placement. Once you have obtained your Approbation, you can directly apply for available Assistenzarzt positions at hospitals and begin your Facharztweiterbildung in your chosen specialty.

Approbation

The Approbation is the full and permanent medical licence in Germany. It allows you to practise medicine independently, without the restrictions of a temporary work permit (Berufserlaubnis).

Read moreShow less

The Approbation is the official state issued licence that allows you to work independently as a doctor in Germany. Unlike the temporary Berufserlaubnis, it has no fixed validity period and is recognised throughout Germany.

Once granted, you can work as an Assistenzarzt, continue your Facharztweiterbildung or pursue other medical career opportunities. Applications are handled by the respective state authorities (Approbationsbehörde), depending on the Bundesland in which you intend to work.

The Approbation also gives you greater professional flexibility and can make it easier to pursue long term career and residence opportunities in Germany. For international doctors, obtaining Approbation is one of the most important milestones in building a medical career in Germany.

Hospitation

A Hospitation is a temporary, usually unpaid opportunity for international doctors to observe daily clinical work in German hospitals. It helps you become familiar with the healthcare system, improve your medical German and gain valuable contacts before starting work in Germany.

Read moreShow less

A Hospitation allows international doctors to shadow experienced physicians and understand how German hospitals function. You work as an observer and are not allowed to independently examine or treat patients, prescribe medication or perform medical procedures.

It usually lasts between one and three months, depending on the hospital. During this time, you can improve your medical German, learn hospital routines and gain practical insights into different departments. It can also help you build professional contacts and obtain a recommendation for future job applications.

Generally, you will need your medical degree, CV, a motivation letter, valid travel documents and sufficient German language skills. Applications are usually sent directly to the Chief of Department (Chefarzt) or the hospital’s HR department.

Completing a Hospitation before obtaining your Berufserlaubnis or Approbation can be a valuable first step towards starting your medical career in Germany.
